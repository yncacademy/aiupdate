04 Length decides the model

Video models: length decides the model

Choose the clip length first, then the model that can actually deliver it. Under 15 seconds, 15 to 30 seconds, cinematic, cheap animation, or a talking head — each has a different winner.

The first decision is not the model, it is the clip length. A 30-second idea in a 5-second model wastes a day. Pick the length, then the tool, then write the prompt to fit it.

04.3Demo: replace someone in a video with you

  1. Pick a short source video you have the right to use.

    Your own footage is the safest. If it is someone else's, you need their permission before you put your face on it.

  2. Upload your clear face photos as references.

    Front-on, even light, no sunglasses, no heavy filter.

  3. Write which person to replace and what stays the same.

    Say what must not change: the scene, the camera move, the audio.

  4. Generate.
  5. Check face, hands, text and audio.

    Hands and on-screen text are where these models still break.

04.4Demo: edit a video with Gemini Omni

Add one effect to an existing clip. The trick is to describe the change per second, not per clip — the model follows a timeline better than an adjective.

04.6Checklist before you generate

  • One main idea per clip.
  • Choose the clip length first, then write the prompt to fit it.
  • Write the full character description in every clip. Best is to use a first-frame image and also paste the first-frame image prompt into the video generator, so the model knows what the image is about.
  • Generate two or three versions and pick the best. Do not accept the first.
  • Check lip sync, hands, text on screen and audio.
  • Add captions in CapCut. Keep them large and low on the screen.

04.7A full example prompt

This is the paper-craft CTA video prompt, kept exactly as written. Use it as a model for how much detail a 15-second clip needs: who speaks, what happens in each beat, and how the sound lands.

Prompt Paper-craft CTA video (15s) 
"Retro digital collage cut-out animation, punk zine aesthetic: layered magazine cutouts with torn-paper edges, halftone dot textures, analog photocopy grain, screen-print imperfections, bold Pop Art color blocks (hot pink, mustard yellow, cobalt blue, newsprint cream), pixel-art accents, and ransom-note typography on aged paper. Center frame holds a reserved collage-framed "screen zone", a roughly pasted rectangle with taped corners and marker annotations around it, where a talking-head presenter video would sit, so the surrounding animation never overlaps that zone. An energetic, friendly male Character speaks the lines below. 0-4s: The collage slams together around the central screen zone: a giant ransom-note headline "NEW AI. NEW STRATEGIES." slams down, with cutout robot heads, chat bubbles, camera icons, film clapperboards, and sparkle stars orbiting it (generic icons only, no official logos or brand names). Character says: "AI changed again, and the best tools are not the ones you think." 4-10s: The orbiting elements reorganize into three pasted index cards that flip down one by one: a paper chat bubble with a pen ("TEXT, IMAGE, VIDEO"), a paper laptop with a play button ("LIVE DEMOS"), and a rising paper chart with a shopping tag ("BLACK FRIDAY PLAN"). Red marker circles and arrows annotate each, and a taped "FREE LIVE TRAINING · SATURDAY" ticket stub is slapped on screen. Character says: "This SATURDAY, I am hosting a live training sharing the best AI tools for text, images, video and agents, plus your Black Friday business plan." 10-12s: A rubber stamp slams "START FREE" diagonally across the frame in ransom-note letters, paper dust puffing. Character says: "Free and no Replay." 12-15s: Everything collapses into a clean poster collage around the screen zone: halftone sunburst, pixel-art stars and shopping bags, and a torn-paper arrow pointing down toward the bottom edge with ransom-note text "LINK IN COMMENTS." Character says: "Link in comments!" Camera stays locked with punchy snap-zooms and paper-layer parallax between beats, like pages slamming onto a photocopier bed. Fast, playful, high-contrast rhythm; each beat lands on a distinct Pop Art color field. Sound: upbeat lo-fi collage beats with paper-rip, flip, and rubber-stamp SFX punctuating each line."
